Projects / European Holiday Sales — SQL Analysis

SQL · Data Analysis

What five years of Christmas shopping across Europe actually tells you

A SQL deep-dive into holiday sales from a European supermarket chain — broken down by country, channel, product, and customer segment to find out what actually moves seasonal revenue, and where the next season's strategy should focus.

Tools SQL Server — CTEs, self-joins, views
Scope 4 seasons (2018–2021) · 10 countries
Dimensions Geography, channel, product, age & gender
Goal Marketing, staffing & channel decisions

Key findings

What the data actually shows, next to what to do about it.

Key Finding

Recommended Action

The youngest shoppers (1–11) behave nothing like teens or adults — they shop almost entirely in-store and pay in cash, while 12–17 and 18+ look nearly identical to each other.

Treat kids as their own market: build family-focused, in-store holiday experiences for them, and run one unified channel strategy for teens and adults.

Profit margin barely moves across all 13 product categories (76.7–77.0%) — Toys' revenue lead is a volume story, not a margin story.

Double down on what already sells: scale Toys and Clothing further instead of chasing "higher-margin" categories.

Orders cluster tightly between 9 AM and 6 PM, peak at 3 PM, then drop sharply after 7 PM.

Concentrate staffing and promotions in that 9-to-6 window and pull back on after-hours spend.

Female shoppers peak on Monday; male shoppers peak on Friday — a real day-of-week split, not noise.

Time gender-targeted campaigns to match: female-focused Monday, male-focused Friday.

Sweden leads total revenue ($3.78M) but actually declined -6.2% last season, while Austria (+12.4%) and the UK (+11.8%) are quietly the fastest-growing markets.

Defend Sweden's position while shifting incremental investment toward Austria and the UK.

In-store held 63–67% of revenue every season — even through 2020, when many retailers saw shoppers move online.

Weigh new digital investment carefully; this customer base hasn't shown it wants to shop online.

Revenue & growth trends

Revenue held remarkably steady across four seasons — a 2020 dip and a full recovery, with profit margin barely moving through any of it.

Question 1 — Revenue, quantity, cost & profit per season

Revenue dipped to $6.95M in 2020 (likely pandemic disruption) before recovering to $7.09M in 2021 — but profit margin held rock-steady at ~77% through every season, dip included. Costs scaled with revenue; nothing structural broke.

Question 2 & 3 — Season-over-season growth

Growth swung from +1.5% (2019) to -3.2% (2020) and back to +1.9% (2021) — a clear dip-and-recovery pattern. In the most recent season, revenue (+1.9%) and quantity (+2.1%) moved almost in lockstep, meaning growth came from more units sold, not higher prices.

Question 6 — Growth ranking, most recent season

Revenue leadership doesn't mean growth momentum. Austria (+12.4%) and the UK (+11.8%) grew fastest in 2021–22, while Sweden — the #1 country by total revenue — actually declined -6.2%, and France fell hardest at -8.4%.

▸ View SQL 
-- Self-join on xmas_year + country to line up each country against its own prior season
;WITH s AS (
    SELECT xmas_year, xmas_season, country, SUM(total_sales) AS sales
    FROM dbo.v_xmas_sales
    GROUP BY xmas_year, xmas_season, country
)
SELECT s.xmas_year, s.country,
    ROUND(s.sales / POWER(10,6), 2) AS sales,
    (s.sales - prev.sales) / prev.sales AS sales_growth_percentage
FROM s
LEFT JOIN s prev ON s.xmas_year = prev.xmas_year + 1 AND s.country = prev.country
WHERE s.xmas_year = (SELECT MAX(xmas_year) FROM dbo.v_xmas_sales)
ORDER BY sales_growth_percentage DESC

Question 7 — Revenue share by age, gender & purchase method

The 1–11 age group drives the single largest share of holiday revenue (38.9%) — edging out both teens (30.8%) and adults (30.3%) individually. Gender split is nearly even (50.6% female / 49.4% male), and in-store still dominates overall (64.7%).

Question 8 — Channel & payment mix within each age group

Teens (12–17) and adults (18+) are nearly indistinguishable in both channel and payment behavior (58.7% vs. 58.3% in-store, 23.6% vs. 23.1% PayPal). The real split isn't teen-vs-adult — it's kids-vs-everyone-else: the 1–11 group shops almost exclusively in-store (74.4%) and pays almost entirely in cash (86.2%).

Question 10 — Weekday & hourly timing

Orders cluster tightly between 9 AM and 6 PM, peak at 3 PM (3,131 orders), then fall off a cliff after 7 PM — a clear "prime shopping window" rather than an even spread across the day. Within the week, female shoppers peak on Monday and male shoppers peak on Friday.

Question 9 — Sales, profit ratio & avg. unit price by category

Toys leads by a wide margin (15.8% of total revenue, nearly 40% ahead of the next category), but profit ratio barely moves across all 13 categories (76.7–77.0%). Profitability here isn't about picking a "better margin" category — it's about volume in what already sells.

DECLARE @total_sales DECIMAL(18,0) = (SELECT SUM(total_sales) FROM dbo.v_xmas_sales);

;WITH s AS (
    SELECT product_category, SUM(total_sales) AS sales,
        AVG(unit_price) AS avg_unit_price,
        SUM(profit) / SUM(total_sales) AS profit_ratio
    FROM dbo.v_xmas_sales
    GROUP BY product_category
)
SELECT product_category,
    ROUND(s.sales / POWER(10,6), 2) AS sales,
    ROUND(sales / @total_sales, 3) AS sales_proportion,
    ROUND(profit_ratio, 4) AS profit_ratio
FROM s
ORDER BY product_category
